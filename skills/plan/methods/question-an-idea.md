# Question an idea

Use when the owner (or you) has a half-formed idea and it must be made solid enough to plan, or dropped with confidence.

## How

- **Scrutinize, do not endorse.** No praise; each answer either challenges a weak point or builds on a strong one.
- **One question at a time**, in dependency order, each with your current best answer when that helps him reply. Find what you can yourself (the code, the data, the vault) instead of asking.
- **Pin the terms.** When a word is fuzzy or overloaded (user, buyer, payer; done, delivered), name the ambiguity and get one meaning.
- **Check claims against the project.** A label or summary is not proof: open the files. If they contradict the idea, resolve that first.
- **Two voices** when it helps: one teammate's view (a role from `bmn team`), one invented outsider (a competitor, a finance reviewer, a skeptical user), each argued in character for a turn, then synthesized into your next question. For an independent view that must not share your reasoning, ask a teammate ([roundtable.md](roundtable.md)).
- **Modes** he can ask for: *attack* (find contradictions, weak assumptions, failure cases; do not agree), *defend* (argue the strongest version), *switch*.

## Record as you go

Keep `plan/references/<idea>/questions.md`: one bullet per decision, assumption, crack (a weakness found), kill (a branch dropped) and lock (a point settled, not to reopen). Locks become the brief the plan starts from.

## Ends with

Either the locked brief (what, for whom, why now, what not, the riskiest assumption and how to test it), ready for [frame.md](frame.md) or an epic, or a clear "drop it" with the reason.
